Account & data safety

An account is optional — it only matters for Sync Mode. In Base and Local mode the app works fully on your device, forever. When you do create one, it exists for one reason: your library follows you between devices.

🔒

Designed to be trustable

  • 🔒 Encrypted in transit & at rest — accounts run on Supabase (PostgreSQL) with TLS and row-level security.
  • 🪪 Your data is only yours — a database policy means only your signed-in session can read or write your row.
  • 🚫 No ads, no analytics, no selling — the app makes no requests beyond your Bible files and your own sync.
  • 📤 Full export, anytime — Tools → Export Library gives your entire library as JSON. Never locked in.
  • 📜 Open-source texts only — KJV, BSB, WEB, ASV and Strong's numbers are public domain.

Create your free account

We ask for an email and a password — nothing else. The email is used for sign-in and password resets only, and is never shared or used for marketing.

Questions people ask

What's the difference between Base, Sync and Local mode?

Base Mode loads the basics once and keeps scripture display working offline — zero cloud contacts. Sync Mode signs you in at start; your library pushes and pulls to your account automatically. Local Mode is the hybrid: everything (Bibles, schedules, lyrics, themes) saves on this device only. Switch anytime from the top bar — none of the modes stop the app from working offline.

Is it really free? Where's the catch?

No catch: the app is static HTML/JS you can host anywhere for free, Bibles are public domain, sync uses Supabase's free tier. No server cost, no subscription — and nothing is sold. Not your data, not anything.

Does it work without internet during a service?

Yes — that's the whole point. Open it once online; the service worker downloads all five Bibles, the starter hymns and the app shell. After that it runs from your device's cache with Wi-Fi off — in every mode. Voice recognition is the one exception (it's a browser feature that needs connectivity).

Who can see my library if I create an account?

Only you. Row-level security means every request is checked against your signed-in identity; the database physically refuses queries for other users' rows. Export your full library to JSON anytime.

Can several people share one account?

You can, but it's not designed that way — the library is per-account. Sign in with the same email on the phone and the laptop and they stay in sync.

What about modern translations like NIV or ESV?

They're copyrighted, so bundling isn't allowed without a publisher license (which is granted to churches). KOGG ships only legally shareable texts; you can still display such a translation from a website they permit. Everything else — themes, songs, plans, the interface — has zero restrictions.
